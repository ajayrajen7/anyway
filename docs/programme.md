# 6-Month Training Programme

**Built around:** right knee (meniscus/cartilage — load fine, impact out), bilateral mid-portion Achilles tendinopathy (no running), groin/lower-abdominal pain (cumulative-load and pressure sensitive), poor global flexibility, flat foot.

**Assumptions** — correct me if wrong: your landmine implies a barbell and plates are available; bands substitute for the missing cables; the 60 minutes includes warm-up and cool-down.

---

## Part 1 — The Rules That Matter More Than The Exercises

These are the difference between this working and this becoming injury number four.

### The breathing rule
**Never hold your breath on a rep. Exhale through the hard part of every single repetition.** If a weight is heavy enough that you instinctively brace and hold, it is too heavy for you right now — regardless of whether you could lift it. Intra-abdominal pressure is your primary aggravator, and breath-holding is what generates it.

Practical version: if you can't talk in short phrases at the top of a set, drop the load.

### The pain traffic light
- **During the session:** discomfort up to 4/10 is acceptable. Above that, stop the exercise, not the session.
- **Next morning:** you should be back to your normal baseline. Not pain-free — back to *your* baseline.
- **If a flare lasts beyond 48 hours:** that session's load drops 20% next time, and you remove the two highest-pressure exercises from it.
- **Three flares in a month:** you're on the wrong phase. Go back one.

Your pain is delayed and cumulative, so you cannot use in-session feel as your guide. You need a log. One line per session: date, what you did, and a 0–10 score the following morning. This is not optional — without it you're guessing, and you've already spent a year guessing.

### Permanently excluded
Conventional and Romanian deadlifts (loaded), heavy back squats, standing barbell overhead press at load, heavy loaded carries, kettlebell swings, hanging leg raises, ab wheel, sit-ups and crunches, hollow holds, box jumps, skipping, burpees, running.

Everything on that list either loads the hinge pattern under brace, or spikes intra-abdominal pressure, or involves impact. Some come back cautiously in Phase 3. Running doesn't.

### The scheduling principle
You currently flare after three consecutive loading days. So the week is built so you never have three. Loading days are separated by a mobility/cardio day. This is why you get six days a week without six hard days.

## Phase 1 — Weeks 1 to 6: Tolerance and Tissue Prep

**Deliberately below your current capacity.** You currently manage 6 exercises at 30 kg goblet. Phase 1 sits under that. The goal here is not stimulus — it's establishing that you can train four times a week without a flare. Until that's proven, adding load is just re-running the last two years.

### Monday — Lower A

**Warm-up (8 min)**
- Short foot exercise — 2 × 30s per side *(arch activation; directly relevant to your flat foot)*
- Knee-to-wall ankle dorsiflexion — 2 × 10 per side
- 90/90 hip switches — 2 × 8
- Glute bridge — 2 × 12
- Bodyweight goblet squat to comfortable depth — 10

**Main**
1. **Leg press** — 3 × 12–15, moderate load, no lockout, feet mid-platform. *This is the highest groin-pressure exercise in the programme. Stop 3 reps short of failure, always. If any single exercise is going to flare you, it's this one — treat load increases here with more caution than anywhere else.*
2. **Goblet squat** — 3 × 12 @ 25 kg *(down from your 30 kg — start conservative)*
3. **Rear-foot-elevated split squat**, low elevation (15 cm) — 3 × 10 per side, bodyweight or 8 kg per hand
4. **Sliding leg curl** (towel on floor) or band leg curl — 3 × 12 *(hamstring exposure one — the second comes Thursday)*
5. **Seated calf raise** (dumbbell on knee, knee bent 90°) — 3 × 15, 3s up / 3s down
6. **Standing calf raise**, straight knee — 3 × 12, 3s up / 3s down

*Exercises 5 and 6 are your Achilles rehab, not accessory work. Bent-knee targets soleus, straight-knee targets gastrocnemius — you need both, and mid-portion tendinopathy responds to heavy slow resistance more reliably than to rest. Some tendon discomfort during these (up to 4/10) is expected and acceptable. This is the one place where discomfort is the treatment.*

**Cool-down (5 min)** — couch stretch 45s per side, supine hamstring strap stretch 45s per side, calf wall stretch 45s per side.

### Tuesday — Upper A (Push)

**Warm-up (5 min)** — band pull-aparts 2 × 15, wall slides 2 × 10, thoracic open books 2 × 8 per side.

**Main**
1. **Dumbbell bench press** — 3 × 10 @ 20 kg
2. **One-arm dumbbell row**, bench-supported — 3 × 12 per side. *Bench-supported deliberately — no standing hinge, no bracing.*
3. **Half-kneeling landmine press** — 3 × 10 per side, light. *Half-kneeling deliberately: it removes the standing brace and forces you to press without generating trunk pressure.*
4. **Band lat pulldown**, anchored overhead, half-kneeling — 3 × 12
5. **Dumbbell lateral raise** — 2 × 15, light. *Shoulder health, not width. At this volume and your protein intake you are not going to get broad — that concern is a decade of dedicated training away.*
6. **Band Pallof press** — 3 × 10 per side, breathing continuously throughout the hold

**Cool-down** — doorway pec stretch, lat stretch on bench.

### Wednesday — Mobility + Zone 2

**Cross trainer — 20 minutes, easy.** You listed running, walking, incline walking and cycling as tested, but not the cross trainer. It's a genuine unknown and potentially your biggest unlock: no impact, no seat pressure, no hip flexion under load. Start at 15 minutes in week 1. If it's clean after two weeks, it becomes your primary cardio and the incline walk becomes secondary.

Then the **full mobility protocol** (Part 4, 25 min).

### Thursday — Lower B

**Warm-up** — same as Monday.

**Main**
1. **Hip thrust**, shoulders on bench — 3 × 12. *Start with bodyweight. If a dumbbell across the hips creates lower-abdominal pressure, switch permanently to single-leg bodyweight hip thrusts rather than fighting it.*
2. **Reverse lunge** — 3 × 10 per side, bodyweight to 8 kg per hand. *Reverse rather than forward: less braking force through the right knee.*
3. **Single-leg Romanian deadlift** — 3 × 8 per side, 8 kg only. *This is the one hinge you keep, and it stays permanently light. You are buying the hamstring stretch, not the load. If it creates any groin sensation, cut it.*
4. **Step-up**, 20–25 cm box, 3-second lowering — 2 × 10 per side *(quad exposure two)*
5. **Seated calf raise** — 3 × 15, slow
6. **Tibialis raise** (heels on floor, toes up, back against wall) — 3 × 20. *Under-trained, directly relevant to both your flat foot and your Achilles.*
7. **Side plank** — 3 × 30s per side

**Cool-down** — as Monday.

### Friday — Upper B (Pull)

**Warm-up** — band pull-aparts, scapular retractions, thoracic rotations.

**Main**
1. **Chest-supported dumbbell row** (incline bench, face down) — 3 × 12
2. **Incline dumbbell press** — 3 × 12 *(chest exposure two)*
3. **Dumbbell pullover** — 3 × 12
4. **Push-ups** — 2 sets, stop 2 reps short *(chest exposure three, low load)*
5. **Band face pull** — 3 × 15
6. **Dumbbell curl** — 3 × 12
7. **Dead bug**, exhaling fully on each extension — 3 × 8 per side

*No carries in Phase 1. They're an intra-abdominal pressure exercise dressed up as a grip exercise.*

### Saturday — Mobility + Incline Walk

**Incline walk — 15 minutes at 4.5 km/h, 12% incline.** Deliberately under your known threshold, because you're now also doing Wednesday cardio. If Wednesday's cross trainer proves clean, you can extend this to 20 minutes from week 4.

Then the **full mobility protocol**.

### Sunday
Off. 20–30 minutes of flat walking. Not training — just movement.

## Phase 2 — Weeks 7 to 14: Build

Enter this phase only if Phase 1 produced **fewer than two flares in six weeks**. If it didn't, repeat Phase 1 at 90% load.

Changes:
- Load up 5–10% on everything, or add 2 reps per set — whichever keeps you 3 reps short of failure
- Add one exercise back to each lifting day (you're now at 7–8 per session)
- **Supersets return, but on upper days only.** Pair Tuesday's exercises 3+4 and Friday's 4+5. Upper-body pairing raises heart rate without raising abdominal pressure. Lower-body supersets stay out.
- Calf raises progress to **single-leg**, 3 × 12 each, slow. This is the main Achilles progression and it matters.
- Cross trainer goes to 25–30 minutes, and one session per fortnight becomes intervals (1 min hard / 2 min easy × 6)
- Add **Spanish squat isometric** (band behind knees, 3 × 30s) to Monday — high-value for patellofemoral loading with almost no joint stress

### Phase 3 — Weeks 15 to 24: Density

Enter only if Phase 2 produced **fewer than one flare per three weeks**.

- Lower-body supersets return: pair split squat with calf raise, step-up with tibialis raise. Never pair leg press with anything.
- One circuit of three exercises returns, on Tuesday only, at 60% of your Phase 2 loads
- **Loaded carries return** — suitcase carry, 12 kg, 20m, 3 sets. Light and one-sided. If the abdominal sensation appears, they leave permanently and you've learned something useful.
- Incline walk to 20 minutes at 13–14%
- Test a single set of front-foot-elevated split squats to explore your knee's current range

By month six you should be at roughly 8 exercises per session with two supersets on upper days and one on lower — close to your old volume, arrived at through a route your body can actually absorb.

## Part 4 — The Mobility Protocol

You can't touch your toes and can't sit in a deep squat. This isn't a side project — for someone with flat feet, knee pain and a stiff lower back, it's arguably the highest-return work in this document. Full version twice a week; the 10-minute version daily.

**Full version (25 min)**

*Ankle and foot*
- Knee-to-wall dorsiflexion — 3 × 10 per side
- Calf stretch, straight knee then bent knee — 2 × 45s each, per side
- Short foot exercise — 3 × 30s per side

*Posterior chain*
- Supine hamstring stretch with strap — 3 × 30s per side *(supine, not standing — protects the lower back)*
- Sciatic nerve slider — 2 × 10 per side. *If your toe-touch limitation is neural rather than muscular, which is common with a stiff lower back, this moves the needle when stretching alone doesn't.*
- Cat-camel — 2 × 10

*Hips*
- Couch stretch — 2 × 45s per side
- Figure-4 glute stretch — 2 × 45s per side
- 90/90 hip switches — 3 × 8
- Adductor rock-back — 2 × 30s. **Go gently. Adductor stretching sits close to your groin symptoms; if it produces any sensation there, cut it entirely.**

*Thoracic and squat pattern*
- Open books — 2 × 8 per side
- Heel-elevated goblet squat hold, light — 3 × 30s. *This is how you rebuild the deep squat: hold the position under light load rather than stretching for it.*

**Daily 10-minute version:** knee-to-wall, calf stretch both variants, supine hamstring, couch stretch, cat-camel, 90/90 switches.

Expect meaningful change at 8–12 weeks, not 3.

## Part 5 — Cardio, Given Your Constraints

Groin, not knee or Achilles, is your cardio limiter. That means the answer is variety and spacing, not one modality done more.

**Weekly target: 100–140 minutes of deliberate cardio, plus steps.**

- Cross trainer (Wed) — 20–30 min. The variable to test first.
- Incline walk (Sat) — 15–20 min. Capped.
- Flat walking — daily.

**Your step count is the single highest-leverage, lowest-risk fat-loss lever you have.** Target 8,000–10,000 per day. Across six months, the difference between 5,000 and 9,000 steps daily is larger than the difference between three and four gym sessions a week, and it costs you nothing in flare risk. If you track one thing outside the gym, track this.

## Part 6 — Nutrition

You said the diet can change after all, which changes the outlook materially.

**Protein: 120–130 g per day.** That's 1.6 g per kg of bodyweight, and it's the evidence-supported floor for retaining muscle in a deficit or adding it at maintenance. At 75 g you were asking your body to build with insufficient material. This is the single biggest change in this document.

**On your metabolism theory.** Partially right, but the mechanism matters. Adaptive thermogenesis after prolonged under-eating is real, but it's typically in the range of 5–15% below what you'd predict — not the 30–40% that "my body burns fewer calories now" implies. The larger contributors to your stall are almost certainly: lost lean mass from the four-month training break, a substantial unconscious drop in daily movement during under-eating, and intake drift over a long diet. The practical conclusion is the same either way, though.

**The sequence:**

*Weeks 1–10 — eat at maintenance.* Track for two weeks to find your genuinely weight-stable intake, then hold there. Protein at 120–130 g. Train as programmed. Weight will be roughly flat; body composition will improve. This will feel like doing nothing. It isn't — you're restoring training capacity and metabolic rate simultaneously, and attempting a deficit from your current adapted state would fail the same way the last year did.

*Weeks 11–24 — modest deficit.* Roughly 300–400 kcal below the maintenance figure you established. Protein stays at 130 g. Expect 0.3–0.4 kg per week.

**Realistic six-month outcome: 4–6 kg of fat lost, lean mass held or slightly up.** Not the 6–8 kg you asked for, but a genuine improvement on my earlier estimate — the protein correction is what bought that. And unlike the last year, you'd arrive there with your training capacity intact.

Weigh yourself daily, use the seven-day average, ignore the individual numbers.

## Part 7 — One Medical Loose End

I'll say this once and then leave it alone, because you've earned the right not to be sent to another doctor.

What you describe — fluid-like sensation, relieved by compression, gradual onset through the day, worse after exertion — is also a textbook description of a **varicocele**. Varicoceles are frequently missed on supine ultrasound and require **standing imaging with Valsalva** to show up. Separately, the palpable something in your abdominal wall warrants **dynamic ultrasound**, also standing, also with Valsalva.

If your scans were done lying down, they may not have ruled out the two most likely explanations for your symptoms. One targeted scan, requested specifically as "standing, with Valsalva, assessing for varicocele and inguinal hernia," would either close the question permanently or change the answer entirely. Both outcomes are worth one appointment.

If it comes back clean: stop looking, and this programme is the answer.
